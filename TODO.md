  - [x] **Bearing-aware silence behaviour for the `ask` banner — designed and built 2026-08-27.** Silence on `DeviationConfirmationNotification` now has a third outcome. It still keeps the old route by default, but a walker who spent the whole 90 s *holding one direction away from the route* gets a walk rebuilt around where they are going.     **Reading a direction off the position stream.** The per-fix `bearing` on `PaceUpdate` — the signal this entry originally pointed at, and the one `PoiAlerter` uses — turned out to be the wrong input. `PoiAlerter` can use it because it only needs "roughly ahead" to suppress an alert, and a wrong answer costs a missed notification. Here it decides whether to rebuild someone's walk, and at ~1 fix/s a walker moves ~1.4 m while a phone's horizontal error in a street is several metres, so consecutive fixes disagree wildly. So the window is cut into **chords of ≥ 20 m of straight-line displacement** (not path length — a walker weaving on the spot must not be handed short, noisy chords), each chord gets one bearing, and the chords are compared. 20 m with ~5 m of error at each end is ~14° of bearing error, which is small enough to test against; 15 m is ~18° and was tried and rejected.

    **Window: 45 s, ≥ 2 chords.** Deliberately *not* `DEVIATION_SUSTAIN_MS`'s 30 s, and the 15 s difference is resolution rather than caution. A deviation verdict arrives complete on every fix, so 30 s only has to outlast a bad fix or two; a *direction* has to be measured and measuring it costs ground. Two 20 m chords need 40 m of **net** displacement, and a walker weaving down a pavement nets well under what their legs cover — at an unhurried 1.0 m/s of real progress, 45 s is 45 m and fits; 30 s is 30 m and fits one chord, and one reading cannot show a direction was *held*. The first cut of this used 30 s / 20 m and rejected every walker who was not dead straight at a brisk 1.4 m/s; shrinking the chord to compensate was the wrong lever and the window was lengthened instead. Ceiling on the window is the banner it answers — 45 s sits well inside the 90 s the question stands, so the verdict is about the walker's last stretch, not the whole excursion. It doubles as the staleness bound: a stream that stopped feeding 45 s ago is describing where the walker *was*.

    **Tolerance: 35°, as a spread between chords, not a deviation from their average.** This is the one that took two attempts and is worth knowing about. Measured against the window's overall heading, a walker who turns 80° mid-window gets an average halfway between the two streets, each chord sits ~40° off it, *and* the chord straddling the turn reports the blend and lands almost on the average — so a corner hides inside a statistic it helped compute. The first version waved an 80° turn through as "consistent" (the test that caught it is still there, named for it). Comparing readings with each other removes the hiding place: two directions 80° apart are 80° apart whatever their mean. The number is bounded below by noise — two 20 m chords carrying ~14° each can differ by nearly 30° on a dead straight walk, so anything under ~30° fails honest walkers — and above by the street corner it must catch. A consequence worth stating: a curve sharper than ~35° across the window reads as a change of direction rather than a direction, so a walker rounding a bend gets no answer. That is the right way to fail, since no answer means today's behaviour.

    **Bearing → request: a forward cone over the kept stops, plus discovery — not a synthetic waypoint.** The waypoint option this entry floated was rejected on inspection: `keepAttractions` reaches the API as `explicitAttractions`, so a fake "go this way" stop would land in `orderedAttractions` and from there into the results list, visit detection, post-walk feedback and preference learning, and hiding it would mean teaching the server about a stop that is not a stop. What is built instead composes with `buildDeviationRebuildRequest` rather than replacing it — same current-position origin, same preserved end anchor, same re-timing — and differs in exactly the two ways a heading actually implies. (1) Kept stops are filtered by `stopsAheadOfHeading` to those within **90°** of the heading. 90° is the definition rather than a tuning: the only question a heading asks of a stop is "is this behind me", and behind is the half-plane, so the boundary is the perpendicular. A stop 85° off is directly to the walker's side — a turn, not a walk back — and dropping it would throw away a stop they can still have. Deliberately wider than `PoiAlerter`'s `DIRECTION_TOLERANCE_DEG` of 70, which decides whether to interrupt someone about a POI they would have to look for. (2) `fillRemainingTime` is **true**, which the plain deviation rebuild forbids. That rule was written for a walker who is *lost* and wants what they already chose redrawn to reach them; this walker is going somewhere on purpose and has just had stops cut out from under them, leaving a hole only discovery can fill.

    **Pins beat the cone.** A pinned stop survives whatever its bearing. A pin is something the walker said; a heading is something inferred from their GPS trace, and when the two disagree — they pinned the cathedral and then walked away from it — the one they typed wins. Same rule the planner already follows in refusing to drop a pin even when it makes the plan infeasible.

  - [x] **Security: no cooldown on deviation-triggered rebuilds — flagged and fixed 2026-08-11.** Unlike the pace path (`REPLAN_COOLDOWN_MS`, 12 minutes), nothing throttled how often a sustained-deviation rebuild could fire. The once-per-excursion latch inside `DeviationMonitor` only ever covered a *single* excursion, and rejoining the route clears it — so a walker moving unpredictably (real erratic movement, a GPS glitch bouncing across the 50 m threshold repeatedly, or someone deliberately hammering the client) could start a fresh excursion every time and retrigger the 30-second sustain window over and over, each firing a real `handleBuildWalk` — Overpass + ORS + Gemini calls, real cost and real latency, not free. Ariel flagged this as a real abuse/DoS vector, not just a polish item. **Now fixed:** `DEVIATION_REBUILD_COOLDOWN_MS` in `deviation-monitor.ts`, **3 minutes**, structurally the same `cooldownUntil` mechanism as `ReplanTrigger`'s. Deliberately not the pace path's 12 minutes: that number is sized to let a 15-minute rolling window refill with samples from the new route, and there is no such window on this side — a deviation verdict arrives complete on every fix, so the only thing worth waiting for is the walker's reaction to the route they just got. Three minutes cuts the worst-case rebuild rate by six (one per three minutes instead of one per thirty seconds), enough to make the loop pointless as an abuse vector, while a walker who takes a genuine second wrong turn a few minutes later is unaffected. Three decisions worth knowing about. **(1) It arms on the decision, not on the rebuild** — `auto`, `ask`-then-confirm and `ask`-then-dismiss all behave identically, mirroring `PaceChecker`, where `ReplanTrigger.evaluate()` arms the cooldown whether or not the walker acts on the banner. A dismissed `ask` made no API call, so cost alone wouldn't require it, but the banner is itself an interruption worth rate-limiting (a walker who just said "I know where I'm going" is the last person who wants asking again two minutes later), and dismissal is invisible from inside the monitor — the callback is fire-and-forget, so conditioning on the answer would need a new callback *and* would leave the cooldown un-armed forever for a walker who simply ignores the banner, which is exactly the loop being closed. **(2) `off` charges nothing** — the cooldown is checked *after* the mode gate, unlike `PaceChecker`, which gates the mode after `evaluate()` has already armed. The two differ because the pace side has a window to clear and this side does not; `off` decides nothing and calls nothing, so charging it would only penalise having the feature switched off, matching the existing reasoning that already leaves the once-per-excursion latch alone under `off`. A window that closes during the cooldown likewise isn't latched as spent: if the walker is still off route when the cooldown lapses, the next fix answers them rather than stranding them on a stale route for the rest of the excursion. **(3) It survives a mid-walk `deviationMode` change**, exactly as the pace cooldown survives a `PaceChecker` settings change — otherwise toggling the setting would be the cheapest possible way to clear it. `reset()` clears it, since a new walk owes nothing to the last. **The passive `OffRouteNotification` badge is completely unaffected** — it is driven straight off `detectDeviation`'s `needsReroute` in `WalkPlannerApp` and never passes through the monitor; it costs nothing and tells the walker something true, so throttling it would make no sense. The cooldown gates the *offer*, never the badge, in the same way the sustain window already did. - [x] **Missing constraint: max distance from the start point at walk end — done 2026-08-06.** New optional `WalkPlanRequest.maxEndDistanceFromOriginMeters`, kept deliberately separate from `radiusMeters` (a walk built entirely from POIs 2 km out can still finish 2 km from the start, on the far side of it). the **first statement in the handler** — ahead of body parsing, ahead of the auth-aware branches in `extract-places`, so a flood of malformed requests is refused on the same terms as a valid one and no signed-in/signed-out behaviour moves. **Ariel's constraint was that it must not cost money**, which rules out Upstash, Vercel's paid Firewall rate-limiting product, and anything else needing a new account or key. **Free was the right call here on the merits, not as a compromise:** the app has 1–2 real signed-in accounts (confirmed this session against the live DB), and an external store bought for that would be infrastructure for traffic that does not exist. **Sliding window, not fixed** — the limits are small enough (10–60) that the log per caller is bounded by the limit itself and costs nothing, whereas a fixed window lets a loop fire a full allowance either side of the reset instant and spend double, which on the Gemini route is a real bill and is exactly the burst shape a loop one quota: ORS caps the *account* at 2000/day, so an abusive caller does not degrade their own service, they take the app off the air for everyone. The minute figure has to clear a legitimate build (one optimization call plus one directions call per leg in `route-planner.ts`, so a ten-stop walk is ~10 requests in seconds, and walkers rebuild); the daily figure is the point of the pair — 400 is a fifth of the account's whole day, so no single address can exhaust it for everybody. `/api/geocode` 60/min, which is not a guess: Nominatim's published usage policy is 1 req/sec, so this is precisely the app's own share of it, and the type-ahead behind it is 500ms-debounced and never comes near. `/api/attractions` 20/min, one call per build. **No daily rule on the last two, deliberately:** Nominatim and Overpass cost nothing and have no cap to exhaust, so the risk there is a burst that trips their limiter and gets the User-Agent blocked, which is entirely a per-minute question. **Keyed on the caller's address only** (`x-forwarded-for` first entry, then `x-real-ip`, then one shared bucket under `next dev`, which sets neither and where local development is one person).
